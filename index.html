180 Degrees Consulting · Market sizing

The question: How many potential users could a student-focused professional networking platform serve in the UAE, and which student segments should it target first?

A four-step framework: Define → Size → Refine → Recommend

  1. 1. Define the target market. Two non-overlapping groups: students in grades 11–12 (the last two years of high school) and university students in the UAE. This is the total eligible population, not a user forecast.
  2. 2. Estimate the population. Gather available total enrollment by school/university. Where a school does not report grade-level enrollment, estimate the share in the final two years using an explicit, consistent assumption. Sum the groups and avoid double-counting.
  3. 3. Refine to a realistic opportunity. Distinguish the full potential market from the number the platform could plausibly reach and attract. As a suggested improvement, test low/base/high sign-up rates instead of treating every student as a customer.
  4. 4. Convert the numbers into recommendations. Compare student concentrations, identify promising institutions or segments for an initial launch, and consider direct campus outreach, school partnerships, and student-led promotion.

Verified recollection: You used enrollment totals for some schools and estimated the last-two-year share when those figures were unavailable. 13 How did you estimate the market size for the student platform? CV / experience
Partly reconstructed~75sDEFINE → SIZE → REFINE → RECOMMEND

We approached it in four steps. First, we defined the market: university students in the UAE and high school students in their final two years. The goal was to understand the potential user base for a student-focused professional networking platform.

Second, we sized that population. We gathered the student-enrollment figures we could find for schools and universities. Some schools only published their total enrollment, not how many students were in the final two years, so we estimated that share using reasonable assumptions and made sure not to double-count students.

Finally, we translated the sizing into a go-to-market discussion: which student groups were most attractive to target first and how the client could reach them, for example through university communities and school partnerships. The value was giving the client a structured view of the opportunity rather than one misleadingly precise number.
